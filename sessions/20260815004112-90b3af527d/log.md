A bird is a warm-blooded, egg-laying vertebrate animal belonging to the class Aves. Key characteristics include:

- **Feathers** – the only animals that have them; used for flight, insulation, and display.
- **Beak/bill** – a toothless, keratin-covered jaw used for eating, grooming, and building nests.
- **Wings** – forelimbs modified for flight (though some birds, like penguins and ostriches, are flightless).
- **Skeleton** – lightweight, often with hollow bones, adapted for flight.
- **Respiratory system** – highly efficient lungs with air sacs, allowing high oxygen intake needed for flight.
- **Reproduction** – birds lay hard-shelled eggs, typically incubated outside the body.
- **Metabolism** – high body temperature and fast metabolism to support activity and flight.

Birds are found on every continent, range enormously in size (from the bee hummingbird to the ostrich), and exhibit diverse behaviors such as migration, complex songs, and elaborate mating displays. Scientifically, birds are considered modern-day dinosaurs, having evolved from theropod dinosaurs during the Mesozoic Era.
